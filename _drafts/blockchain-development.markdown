Right now blockchain as a technology is in a place that seems to be really
positive. The regulatory environment around blockchain, and many of the
projects in the ecosystem along with it, is in a fantastic place compared to
where it was a few years ago. And that's really greased the wheels for the
decentralized finance set of services to surge forward. Stablecoins are doing
fantastic. Their use in the very simple form of helping people in areas with
unstable local currency is of immediate value to a few large groups of people.
And they've proven their durability and reliability enough that the new defi
applications being built out now can lean on them to shield users from the
currency valuation risk of being on a blockchain platform while getting all
the benefits of the decentalized platform. I'm not an expert commentator on
finance or blockchain as a technology or cryptography - there are plenty of
places that can explain that stuff better than I can. I just want to frame the
conversation a bit however. Cause while all that stuff is going amazingly well
right now, there are some parts that really seem to be lagging behind and need
some help.

At first I thought maybe I just hadn't gotten deep enough into the current
environment to understand how the parts are supposed to fit together to make a
coherent system. But then I see other people saying things that make a ton of
sense to me, like
[Vitalik's Speech at ETHcc 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jznCAlGknIo).
So maybe it's true that I have a lot more learning to do before I can comment
with authority on lots of the stuff in blockchain. But that doesn't mean that
the "beginners mind" ideas that have popped up so far aren't valid and useful.
While it's been years since I've tried to chew on ideas in public to make
sense of a big platform shift, maybe it's time to do it again. I'll try to be
a little less snarky this time around.

I have done some blockchain development in the past. A few years ago we had
been poking around with ideas built on chain. We didn't end up going that way,
but I did learn enough to do the basics of Ethereum dev using Truffle and
Ganache. And enough Solidity to be able to parse info out of transactions for
well known contracts. Those tools are pretty out of date though, so the first
part was to just bring myself up to date on the development side. There are
some great resources out there. Check out either
[Alchemy University](https://www.alchemy.com/university) or
[Cyfrin Updraft](https://www.cyfrin.io/updraft). As an experienced dev just
looking to lay down a framework for how things are meant to work in web3,
they both do a great job.

But getting up to speed with Hardhat and Ethers is just the first very
simple bit. Next I started trying to find an interesting project to get
involved with. Surely there's a great project out there that I can apply
myself to.
I'm bought into the vision of the next generation of web services being open,
decentralized, and censorship resistant. With significantly north of $100
billion total value locked in defi instruments, the platform part of
blockchains should have made some real substantial progress. But I just can't
find much of it. Of course there are the decentralized finance projects,
they're doing great. And there are plenty of development platform and tooling
projects, cause with defi doing well there's money to grab servicing that 
segment of the market and people building for it.

But where are the apps for users? It all seems to be memecoins still. And do
we really need more of those? A few years ago I could mostly justify all that
token based community stuff as necessary evolution of the practices around a
new technology. But now whenever I see token gating it reminds me of the worst
parts of gatekeeping in gaming, just with very little of the upside and more
rigid system for enforcement. I know it's still a big driver of interest for a
lot of people, it's just not something that interests me at all.

I figured I could end run the whole thing though, cause I remembered there was
a project that had an immediate and obvious usage. And it happened to be tied
to a distribution and access funnel that puts the adoption numbers for just
about any other crypto project to shame. The
[Basic Attention Token](https://basicattentiontoken.org/) is a project from
the Brave browser meant to rewire the connections between the users,
advertisers, and content creators. This is a thing I care about very much. I
also recently swapped my desktop system to full time Linux again, something
that I haven't done in years. And Brave had already become my daily driver
browser on my desktop. The browser itself is fantastic. The Basic Attention
Token has left me scratching my head a bit.
